How to reconcile an auction escrow account

Short answers for SaleLedger, the Windows app that checks an auction trust (escrow) account each month. Each answer has numbered steps. SaleLedger checks records and prepares a report. It does not provide accounting or legal advice and does not file anything on your behalf.

How do I download SaleLedger for Windows?

SaleLedger is a Windows app. There are two ways to install it.

From the Microsoft Store (recommended)

  1. Open SaleLedger in the Microsoft Store.
  2. Install it. Microsoft checks and signs Store apps, so Windows installs it without an unknown-publisher warning.
  3. Open SaleLedger from the Start menu.

With the installer

  1. Download SaleLedger-Setup.exe.
  2. Run it once. It puts a SaleLedger icon on your desktop.
  3. Double-click that icon to open SaleLedger.
The installer isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may show a "Windows protected your PC" notice the first time. Choose More info → Run anyway. The Store version does not show this notice.

Either way, SaleLedger opens in your web browser at 127.0.0.1:5151. That page is served by your own computer, not by us. If SaleLedger is already running, opening it again just opens the browser tab again. To close the app, use the Quit SaleLedger button at the bottom of the page.

Auction Flex reconciliation: how to bring in your records and bank statement

SaleLedger has two ways to bring in records. Most auction houses should use Option B: one trust ledger CSV in SaleLedger's format, plus the trust account bank statement. If you have an .IIF file of your trust account's checks, deposits and transfers (some tools can produce one), use Option A. Otherwise use the ledger template (Option B). For more on each file, see the files behind a trust reconciliation.

QuickBooks Desktop can import IIF files. Intuit says it can't export transactions as IIF. So you may not have an IIF file to give SaleLedger. Option B needs none.

Option B — the trust ledger CSV (start here)

You choose two files: your ledger CSV and your bank statement. In the app, the ledger box is called Auction ledger / settlement export (CSV). It must be a ledger in the format below. An Auction Flex settlement export won't work in that box.

  1. Download the ledger template (CSV). Open it in Excel or any spreadsheet program.
  2. Keep the first row exactly as it is. Those are the headings.
  3. Delete the four example rows. Each description starts with "EXAMPLE".
  4. Add one row for each money event in the trust account for the period, as described below.
  5. Save it as CSV, not as .xlsx.

The columns

The first five headings are required. If one is missing, the file is refused and the message names it.

  • dateRequired. The day the money moved, or the day a pending settlement was worked out. Write 2026-08-31 (best), 08/31/2026 or 08-31-2026. A date like 04/05/2026 is read as April 5.
  • auction_idRequired. The auction number, for example 246. It ties payouts to the buyer funds of the same auction.
  • typeRequired. One of the four values below, spelled exactly. A row with any other value is not counted.
  • partyRequired. Who the money went to or came from. For a consignor payout, write the name as it shows on the bank statement. If the amounts don't match, SaleLedger looks for this name in the bank description.
  • descriptionRequired. A short note, for example "Settlement payout - Auction 246". It can appear in exception messages.
  • gross_amountFor a buyer receipt: the amount deposited. For a consignor row: the consignor's gross sales.
  • commission_amountFor a consignor row: the commission in that settlement. For a commission transfer: the amount moved out of trust.
  • expense_amount, buyback_amountOptional, for your own record. Option B does not use them in its math. Put the result in net_amount.
  • net_amountFor a consignor row: what the consignor is paid or owed after commission, expenses and buybacks.
  • authorized_refA settlement or authorization reference, for example STL-246. Put the same reference on the consignor rows and on the commission transfer that covers them. If a paid consignor row has commission and no commission transfer has the same reference, the report flags it.
  • funds_available_dateFor a buyer receipt: the day the deposit cleared. A consignor paid before the last of these dates for the same auction is flagged as paid early.
  • check_numberFor a consignor payout: the check number. It goes into the report's check register. Leave it blank for a wire.

Amounts: write them as positive numbers, for example 27200.00. $ signs and commas are fine. A minus sign or brackets make an amount negative, so don't use them in the ledger. Leave unused amount cells blank.

The four type values

  • buyer_receiptBuyer money deposited into trust. Fill in gross_amount (the deposit) and, if you know it, funds_available_date. Use one row per bank deposit, because each row is matched to one deposit.
  • consignor_disbursementA settlement paid to a consignor. Fill in net_amount (the amount paid), gross_amount, commission_amount, authorized_ref and check_number.
  • commission_transferCommission moved from trust to your operating account. Fill in commission_amount (the amount moved) and authorized_ref.
  • consignor_settlement_pendingA settlement worked out but not paid yet. Fill in net_amount (what is owed). These rows make up the consignor subledger total. One older than the stale funds threshold on the as-of date is flagged. When you pay it, change the row to consignor_disbursement with the payment date and check number.

Where the rows come from

You copy records you already keep into the template. You don't need new software.

  • Deposit slips or the bank statement give each buyer_receipt: the date and the amount deposited.
  • Each consignor settlement sheet gives that consignor's gross, commission and net. Use your Auction Flex settlement report or your paper sheets.
  • The trust checkbook or check stubs give the date and check number of each payout. Paid settlements become consignor_disbursement rows. Unpaid ones become consignor_settlement_pending rows.
  • Your transfer records (online banking or the checkbook) give each commission_transfer.

The easiest way is to add rows as you go: one when you deposit, one when you write a settlement check, one when you move commission. At month end, save the file as CSV and run it with that month's trust statement. The template's four example rows show one of each type.

Option A — only if you already have an .IIF file of your trust account

If you have an .IIF file of your trust account's checks, deposits and transfers (some tools can produce one), use Option A; otherwise use the ledger template (Option B). You choose three files:

  • Settlement export (CSV)Consignor Summary, Totals by Consignment Order, or Totals by Item, saved as CSV. The first row must have the headings auction_id, consignor, gross_amount and commission_amount. A Consignment Order file also needs co_number; an item file also needs lot_number. expense_amount and buyback_amount are used when present. SaleLedger adds up each consignor's rows and works out the net owed.
  • IIF fileIn the app, the box is called QuickBooks / IIF export for the same auction. SaleLedger reads its CHECK, DEPOSIT and TRANSFER lines. Each consignor's check must carry the same name as in the settlement export (capital letters don't matter). Deposits named BUYERS count as buyer payments. Transfers named HOUSE count as commission transfers.
  • Bank statementYour trust account statement as CSV, Excel (.xlsx), or OFX/QFX. See the bank statement rules below.

Even with an IIF file, SaleLedger counts only deposits named BUYERS and transfers named HOUSE. A deposit or transfer with any other name is left out of your records and can show up as an unmatched bank line. Option A has no pending settlements, so it never reports stale funds. If you have no IIF file, use Option B.

Bank statement rules (both options)

  • A CSV or Excel statement needs a date column, a description column and one amount column. Headings such as Date, Transaction Date or Posting Date; Description, Memo or Details; and Amount or Transaction Amount are recognized.
  • Deposits are positive amounts. Withdrawals are negative, either with a minus sign or in brackets. $ signs and commas are fine.
  • Dates like 2026-08-31 or 08/31/2026 work.
  • In an Excel file, SaleLedger reads the sheet that was open when the file was saved. The first non-empty row is the headings.
  • Use the trust account statement only, not the operating account. A PDF statement can't be read.

Auction trust account reconciliation, step by step

  1. Open SaleLedger and find Option B (or Option A if you have an IIF file).
  2. Choose your files in the file boxes.
  3. In Auction ID/number, type the auction number (Option A only).
  4. In Opening trust balance ($), type the trust account's bank balance on the first day of the period. It can't be negative; use 0.00 if the account was empty.
  5. In Reconciliation as-of date, type the date as YYYY-MM-DD. It starts as today's date.
  6. Option B only: Stale funds threshold (days) starts at 14. Change it if you want.
  7. Click Run reconciliation. The report appears below the forms.

How to read the result

The report starts with one status: IN BALANCE or OUT OF BALANCE. Under it are the three figures that have to agree:

  • Bank adjusted balanceYour opening balance plus every amount on the bank statement.
  • Trust control balanceYour opening balance plus buyer receipts, minus consignor payouts and commission transfers, from your records.
  • Consignor subledger totalYour opening balance plus settlements still owed to consignors.

The month is IN BALANCE only when all three are exactly equal. That means the records you gave SaleLedger agree with each other. It does not mean every legal requirement has been met.

Next month

SaleLedger fills in the opening balance from your last run's bank ending balance. Check it against your actual bank statement and change it if it is different. Then choose the new month's files and click Run reconciliation.

Trust account doesn't match the bank statement? How to read the exceptions

This is where you find missing auction payments and consignor payout mismatches.

Every gap SaleLedger finds is listed under Exceptions, numbered, in plain English. If there are none, it says "No exceptions found." The kinds of exception are:

  • RECEIPT SHORTFALLA buyer receipt in your records was larger than the bank deposit it was matched to.
  • UNMATCHED RECEIPTA bank deposit with no matching auction or consignor entry in your records.
  • DISBURSEMENT MISMATCHA consignor payout cleared the bank for a different amount than your records show. It says whether it was an overpayment or an underpayment.
  • UNMATCHED DISBURSEMENTMoney left the trust account with no matching entry or authorization in your records.
  • AMBIGUOUS MATCHMore than one bank line matches a consignor's surname and none matches the full name. You confirm which one is right.
  • UNAUTHORIZED DEDUCTION REVIEWCommission is part of a consignor's settlement, but no matching authorized commission transfer was found.
  • PREMATURE DISBURSEMENTA consignor was paid before the buyer funds for that auction were available.
  • STALE FUNDSMoney owed to a consignor has not been paid out for longer than the stale funds threshold. This uses Option B's pending settlements.

Each exception names the amount, the date and the consignor or bank line. SaleLedger identifies the problem; you decide the fix. It never assumes an explanation.

The report ends with a Check Register: date, check number, payee and amount for each consignor payout and commission transfer. A payment with no check number shows "(wire/transfer)".

How to fix a problem SaleLedger flags

SaleLedger does not change your files or your books. You make the correction where the record lives, then run the month again.

  1. Read the exception or the red message at the top of the page. It names the amount, date and party involved.
  2. Look up that item in your bank statement, your ledger, your settlement sheets or your checkbook.
  3. Decide with your bookkeeper or accountant what the correction is, and make it in the source system.
  4. Fix the ledger or export the files again, then click Run reconciliation.

Every run is added to your history. If you run the same as-of date twice, both runs are kept.

Common messages

  • Could not find a matching QuickBooks check for: …Option A only. Those consignors are left out of the reconciliation. Make the consignor name the same in the settlement export and the IIF file, then try again.
  • … check(s) in the QuickBooks file didn't match any consignorOption A only. A check's payee name isn't in the settlement export. Check the spelling and that both files are for the same auction.
  • Could not read your files: …A file is missing a required heading, or a date or amount can't be read. The message names the file, the row or the headings it found. Fix the heading or value and try again.
  • Invalid input: …The opening balance isn't a dollar amount or is negative, or the date isn't YYYY-MM-DD.
  • Couldn't reach the license service …Connect to the internet and reload the page.
  • That license key isn't recognized.Check the key and try again. Use Use a different key to type a new one.
  • SaleLedger hit a problem it didn't expect …Nothing was changed. Email the line shown to support@getsaleledger.com.

How to save, print or export the reconciliation report

Above the report are these buttons:

  • Save report (HTML)Downloads the full report as a web page you can open later.
  • Save as textDownloads the report as a plain text file.
  • Export CSVDownloads the status, the three balances, the exceptions and the check register as one CSV you can open in Excel.
  • Print / save as PDFOpens your browser's print window. Choose a printer, or save as PDF.
  • Email reportOpens your own email program with the report in the message. A long report is cut short, so attach the saved file too.
Keep a copy with the month's records. The app does not store past reports. Saved files are named with the as-of date, for example SaleLedger-Reconciliation-2026-08-31.html.

How to back up SaleLedger or move it to another computer

Your data lives on your computer, so backing it up is your job. SaleLedger keeps its own files in one folder: C:\Users\your-name\.saleledger. It holds your license key, your agreement record, and history.json, the list of every reconciliation you ran.

Back up

  1. Copy the .saleledger folder to your backup drive or folder.
  2. Also back up the reports you saved and the source files you used. SaleLedger does not keep copies of them.

Move to another computer

  1. Install SaleLedger on the new computer (see above).
  2. Copy the .saleledger folder into your user folder on the new computer, at the same place.
  3. Open SaleLedger. If it asks, agree and enter your license key again.

Copying the folder keeps your history and the carried-forward opening balance. Your subscription covers your business's own computers.

Common questions

How much do I owe my consignors?

Your settlement sheets say what each consignor is owed. In Option B, you write each consignor's net in the ledger's net_amount column. In Option A, SaleLedger works out the net from the settlement export: gross, minus commission, expenses and buybacks. Either way, it checks that net against the payment that cleared the bank and flags any difference. The report's Consignor subledger total is your opening balance plus the consignor_settlement_pending rows in an Option B ledger. Option A has no pending rows.

Which consignor settlement report do I need?

For Option B, use whichever settlement sheet you already have. You copy each consignor's gross, commission and net into the ledger CSV. For Option A, the settlement file is an Auction Flex export: any one of Consignor Summary (Reports #21/#23), Totals by Consignment Order (#24/#25) or Totals by Item (#26/#27), saved as CSV. Option A also needs an IIF file of the trust account. The headings each file needs are listed above.

What are the auctioneer escrow account rules?

They differ by state. Most states don't require a periodic filing; they reserve the right to examine your trust account. Read your own state's auctioneer rules or ask your licensing board. The report's check register includes the fields named in Wisconsin SPS 125.12(1). Whether your records meet your state's rules is for you and your accountant to determine. Some state rules are summarized on the home page.

Does SaleLedger do auction house bookkeeping?

No. It does monthly trust-account reconciliation and nothing else. General bookkeeping, invoicing and payroll stay in QuickBooks or your own system. SaleLedger checks records; it does not provide accounting or legal advice and does not file anything on your behalf.
